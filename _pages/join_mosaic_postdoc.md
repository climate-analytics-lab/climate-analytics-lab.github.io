**Principal Investigators:** [Dr. Duncan Watson-Parris](https://dwatsonparris.scrippsprofiles.ucsd.edu/) and [Dr. Rose Yu](https://roseyu.com/)

## About the role

We are seeking highly motivated Postdoctoral Researchers to join an interdisciplinary initiative at the intersection of machine learning and Earth system science. The successful candidates will play a central role in developing the next generation of Earth system models (ESMs) by harnessing emerging agentic AI approaches to automate the discovery and improvement of atmospheric parameterizations.

A key component of this work is the recently Google-funded [MOSAIC]({{ '/projects/mosaic/' | relative_url }}) project, which combines observations, symbolic equation discovery and differentiable simulation, and rigorously evaluates and tests models against observations at scale using [ClimateBench2](https://github.com/climate-federation/ClimateBench2). Beyond the core project, the role offers the flexibility to broadly shape the future of AI for climate science.

## Position highlights

- **Guaranteed stability:** a secure, 2-year guaranteed appointment.
- **Excellent compensation:** a competitive salary commensurate with expertise.
- **Interdisciplinary collaboration:** you will work directly with leading machine learning experts and climate scientists, bridging research across international modeling groups.
- **Work environment:** a beautiful location, collaborating across the Scripps Institution of Oceanography and the Department of Computer Science and Engineering.

## Key responsibilities

- Develop agentic AI pipelines that autonomously discover and refine parameterizations within a differentiable ESM ([JAX-GCM]({{ '/projects/jaxgcm/' | relative_url }})), learning from observational data across scales.
- Leverage and curate cloud-hosted observational datasets (including CMIP and Obs4MIPs) to test model performance on historical changes and paleo-climate periods.
- Define and test physicality protocols to ensure AI models conserve energy and obey physical relationships under unseen conditions.
- Ensure research impact by adhering to open-science principles, making all assembled datasets, code pipelines, and publications publicly available.

## Qualifications

- Ph.D. in Computer Science, Climate Science, Physics, Data Science, or a closely related field. Experience with differentiable programming (JAX, PyTorch) is a plus.
- Strong foundation in machine learning (e.g., deep learning, generative models, agentic/LLM-based systems) and/or Earth system modeling.
- A demonstrated passion for addressing climate change and developing open, trustworthy AI systems.
